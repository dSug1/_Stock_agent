I'm not a lawyer, so this is informational, not legal advice. Here's the situation for each data source in your project:

Yahoo Finance (yfinance)

Yahoo Finance does not offer a public API with clear commercial terms. The yfinance Python library is an unofficial scraper — Yahoo has no official agreement with it.
Their Terms of Service prohibit redistribution and commercial use of their data.
Profit or not: technically you're violating their ToS by serving their data to third parties through your app. Many small projects do this, but Yahoo can block you or take legal action at any time.
For legitimate commercial use, you'd need a licensed market data provider (e.g., Polygon.io, Alpha Vantage, Twelve Data, IEX Cloud — all have paid tiers with redistribution rights).
Finviz

I don't see Finviz being used in your current code. If you add it later, same principle: their ToS prohibit scraping and redistribution.
TradingView Lightweight Charts

Licensed under Apache 2.0 — free for commercial use, no royalties. You're fine here.
Three.js

Licensed under MIT — free for commercial use, no royalties. You're fine here.
Summary:

Source	Free use?	Commercial use?
Three.js	Yes (MIT)	Yes
TradingView Lightweight Charts	Yes (Apache 2.0)	Yes
Yahoo Finance data via yfinance	Grey area	No — violates ToS
If you plan to deploy publicly, the main risk is Yahoo Finance data. You should switch to a licensed data provider with explicit redistribution rights. Most offer free tiers for non-commercial/low-volume use and affordable paid tiers for commercial use.


If you want to go legit, the cheapest options for using financial data with redistribution rights:

Polygon.io — free tier (5 API calls/min), paid from $29/mo
Twelve Data — free tier (800 calls/day), paid from $29/mo
Alpha Vantage — free tier (25 calls/day), paid from $50/mo
These give you a proper API with explicit permission to display the data to your users.
