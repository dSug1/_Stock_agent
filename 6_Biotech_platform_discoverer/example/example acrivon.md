Acrivon Therapeutics  
  
This is the full KaiSR poster (C053) from Acrivon Therapeutics — the AI/computational core of the AP3 platform. It’s worth a careful parse because KaiSR is, in many ways, the part of the platform that determines whether AP3 scales from “clever assay” to “drug-discovery engine,” and this poster is the first detailed public characterization of how it actually works. Let me work through what it establishes, where the rigor is genuine, and where the honest caveats sit.  
What KaiSR Actually Is — and the Problem It Solves  
KaiSR exists to solve a specific, real bottleneck that the poster states plainly in the Background: most phosphosites detected by mass spectrometry have no known kinase assigned to them. You can measure 120,000 phosphosites in a cell, but if you don’t know which kinase put each phosphate there, you can’t infer which kinases are active or how a drug perturbed the signaling network. The poster says the vast majority of MS-detected phosphosites lack annotated kinase-substrate relationships (KSRs). That’s the gap. Without filling it, AP3 phosphoproteomics is a list of phosphosites without a wiring diagram; with it, AP3 becomes a readout of kinase activity across the whole kinome.  
KaiSR is the AI that fills the gap. Architecturally, it’s two transformer models fine-tuned on ESM-2 (Meta’s protein language model — the same class of model lineage as AlphaFold-adjacent protein LMs). Model I is trained on PhosphoSitePlus KSRs (AUPRC 0.83); Model II on the iKiP-DB in-vitro kinase database (AUPRC 0.92). It’s an ensemble of the two. The key capability claim is zero-shot prediction — it can infer substrates for kinases it wasn’t explicitly trained on, by learning the general “grammar” of how kinases recognize substrate sequences from the ESM-2 protein-language foundation.  
What the Poster Genuinely Establishes — Four Real Results  
1. Massive KSR expansion. This is the headline scale claim. KaiSR expanded the known substrate sets enormously: from 313 curated CHEK1 substrates (PSP/iKiP) to 3,399 KaiSR-predicted CHEK1 substrates, and predicted 2,314 WEE1 substrates. For a kinase-targeted drug company, this matters directly — CHEK1 is ACR-368’s target and WEE1 is ACR-2316’s target. A 10x expansion of the known substrate network for your own drugs’ targets means AP3 can read those drugs’ effects across vastly more of the signaling network than the public databases allow. The expansion is the asset.  
2. Outperformance versus competing tools. The poster benchmarks KaiSR against Phosformer and KolossuS (other published kinase-prediction AI tools) and a motif-based approach, and claims KaiSR correctly infers kinase activities more often across three independent public drug-perturbation datasets. This is the right kind of validation — using external, public perturbation data (not Acrivon’s own) to test whether KaiSR’s predicted kinase activities match known drug effects. That’s a meaningful, less-home-field-advantaged test than the company’s own xenografts.  
3. The ACR-2316 differentiation, computationally. This panel directly connects to the A097 abstract you shared last turn. KaiSR was used to compare inferred kinase activities across adavosertib, lunresertib, adavosertib+lunresertib, and ACR-2316 — and it’s the computational engine that detected ACR-2316’s distinctive CDK1/CDK2/PLK1 activation signature versus the benchmarks. So the PLK1-differentiation story (proven causally by rescue experiments in A097) is also the thing KaiSR independently flags from the phosphoproteomic data. The two posters corroborate each other: A097 proves PLK1 matters causally; C053 shows KaiSR can detect the PLK1 difference computationally. That cross-validation is genuinely strengthening.  
4. The InViKA validation loop — the most rigorous element. This is the part that elevates the poster above “we built a model and it predicts things.” InViKA (In Vitro Kinase Assay) is a wet-lab ground-truth check: they took recombinant WEE1 and PLK1 kinases, let them phosphorylate substrates in vitro, identified the actual substrates by MS (volcano plots), and then asked whether KaiSR’s predictions matched the experimentally-determined substrates. The statistics shown — WEE1 hypergeometric P-value 9.07e-65, PLK1 6.34e-13, with significant odds ratios — indicate KaiSR’s predicted substrates are strongly enriched in the experimentally-confirmed substrate sets. This is the model being checked against fresh wet-lab truth, not just against held-out database entries. That’s the difference between a model that memorized a database and one that captures real kinase biology.  
The FLT4 Result — Why It’s the Most Strategically Important Panel  
The “target identification using real-world data” section is the one that matters most for the platform-value thesis, and it connects to something mentioned earlier in this conversation. KaiSR was applied to CPTAC (public tumor proteomic data with matched survival) to do de novo target discovery. In lung squamous cell carcinoma (LSCC), it inferred kinase activities across patients and flagged FLT4 as a top candidate — then showed that FLT4-high patients have significantly worse survival (P = 2.2e-05; HR = 0.29, CI [0.11, 0.78]).  
Why this matters: this is KaiSR functioning not as a drug-effect readout but as a target-discovery engine — mining public data to find a kinase whose activity stratifies patient survival, which is the first step toward a new drug program. It demonstrates the platform can originate hypotheses (new targets), not just characterize existing drugs. That’s the generative-platform claim that, throughout this conversation, has been the open question separating “AP3 repositions drugs” (proven by ACR-368) from “AP3 originates drugs” (the higher-value claim). FLT4 is a concrete instance of origination — albeit a computational hypothesis, not yet a drug.  
Note the HR is favorable-direction labeled (HR=0.29 for the reference group), and the confidence interval [0.11, 0.78] excludes 1.0 — so the survival stratification is statistically real in this dataset. As an inferred parameter from a single retrospective public dataset, it’s hypothesis-generating, and the wide CI (a 7-fold range on the hazard ratio) reflects the modest patient numbers — exactly the kind of inferred-parameter-with-wide-credible-interval that warrants treating as a signal to pursue, not an established fact.  
The Honest Caveats — Where Discipline Is Required  
Three things to weight against the impressive results:  
First, this is a company-authored poster validating the company’s own proprietary model. The benchmarks against Phosformer/KolossuS, the InViKA loop, and the CPTAC application are all real and reasonably designed — but they’re run and reported by Acrivon, comparing Acrivon’s tool favorably. The InViKA wet-lab validation is the most independent element (it’s checking against fresh experimental truth), which is why it’s the most credible. The competitor benchmarks should be read with the awareness that the people who built KaiSR designed the comparison.  
Second, the AUPRC numbers need context. AUPRC 0.83 and 0.92 on held-out data are good, but held-out performance on the same database the model was trained on (PSP, iKiP-DB) partly measures the model’s ability to recapitulate database structure, not necessarily its real-world predictive validity. This is the standard concern with any model evaluated on held-out splits of its training source. The InViKA wet-lab check is what addresses this — it’s the test that the held-out AUPRC numbers can’t provide on their own. So the AUPRC figures are reassuring but the InViKA enrichment is the more meaningful validation.  
Third — and most important for the moat thesis — KaiSR’s architecture is now substantially public. This poster discloses the architecture (two ESM-2-fine-tuned transformers, ensemble, zero-shot), the training sources (PSP, iKiP-DB — both public databases), and the performance. As discussed in the patent-moat analysis earlier, this means the architecture is replicable — a competent ML team with ESM-2 and the public KSR databases could build something architecturally similar. What remains proprietary, and what this poster does not disclose, is the 120,000-phosphosite in-house dataset the kinome-wide KSR network was built from, and the model weights. The poster says the kinome map was “derived from 120,000 phosphosites identified in in-house phosphoproteomic experiments” — that proprietary experimental dataset, generated from Acrivon’s acute-drug-exposure profiling, is the actual moat. The model architecture is the commoditizable part (like MaxQuant/Andromeda discussed earlier); the proprietary drug-response training data is the defensible part. This poster, by disclosing the architecture while withholding the dataset, actually illustrates exactly where Acrivon draws the patent-vs-trade-secret line.  
The Synthesis  
KaiSR (C053) is the computational engine that turns AP3 from a phosphosite-measuring assay into a kinome-wide activity-inference and target-discovery platform. The poster establishes four genuine things: large-scale KSR expansion (10x on ACR-368’s and ACR-2316’s own targets), outperformance versus published competitor tools on external data, computational corroboration of the ACR-2316/PLK1 differentiation that A097 proved causally, and a wet-lab InViKA validation loop that checks predictions against fresh experimental ground truth (the most rigorous element). The FLT4/CPTAC result is the strategically important one — it shows KaiSR can originate target hypotheses from public data, which is the generative-platform capability that matters for the acquisition/platform-value case.  
Under the discipline applied throughout: this is strong evidence that the computational layer of AP3 is real, sophisticated, and capable of both reading drug effects and generating new target hypotheses. It is company-authored validation, the held-out AUPRC numbers carry the usual same-source caveat (mitigated by the InViKA wet-lab check), and it confirms that the durable moat is the proprietary 120,000-phosphosite dataset, not the now-disclosed model architecture. KaiSR strengthens the platform-validation and acquisition-optionality thesis — a buyer acquiring Acrivon gets a working kinome-wide target-discovery engine plus the proprietary data it runs on — but like the other platform evidence, it contributes to long-term platform value rather than the near-term ACR-368-anchored catalyst thesis. It’s the strongest single piece of evidence that AP3 is a genuine generative discovery platform and not just a companion-diagnostic assay.
